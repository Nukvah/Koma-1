3 · The Harbor Fire

The bell on the breakwater rang twice before anyone on the quay looked up. By then the smoke had already found the gap between the warehouses and was rolling out over the water, low and grey, the way fog came in on winter mornings.

Mirelle set down the coil of rope she had been carrying and counted the boats still tied at the far pier. Seven. There had been nine at dusk.

“Someone cut them loose,” said the ferryman behind her, not quite a question. She did not answer. On the other side of the harbor a light came on in the keeper's tower, then went out again, then came on and stayed.
